# How I Use AI

## My policy

I use AI to help me organize my thoughts and simplify concepts so they're easier to adapt and understand. I have an ethical barrier: is this lack of transparency appropriate or inappropriate towards my professors?

That question is the one Make 6 forced on me, and the rest of this page is the record I keep so the answer is always checkable: every make has a dated log that says what I asked for, what the AI produced, and what I kept, changed, or threw out. The logs are in the [AI-use log folder](ai-log/), and each make page links to its own.

## What AI tools I have used

- **Perplexity Computer (Claude)** on every make since the selfie, as a tutor, editor, formatter, and, on Makes 6 and 7, as an agent that researched, wrote and built the whole artifact on its own.
- **Image models** for the makes that asked for images: Canva AI for the selfie (Make 2), image generation for the comic panels (Make 3) and the six frames of the GIF (Make 4).
- **GPT 6.1 Sol** as the thing being studied in Make 5, run against Voyant on the same poem.
- **Tracery** (Make 8) and **Twine/Harlowe** (Make 9), which are not AI: a grammar-based randomizer and an interactive-fiction format. The AI part of those two makes was the assistant's help, not the tool.

## Specific prompts and tasks, by make

- **Make 2 – AI Selfie:** asked Canva AI to turn a casual photo into a corporate headshot, then annotated what it changed (suit, studio background, removed details). The remix was mine.
- **Make 3 – Comic:** prompted images of a goalie from inside the mask, critiqued what the model got wrong about the position, re-prompted, and used the mistakes as the comic's argument.
- **Make 4 – GIF:** one prompt, six unchanged outputs, animated to show the model's templates. The assistant assembled the GIF and drafted the reflection from my decisions.
- **Make 5 – Text analysis:** asked GPT to read "The Raven" four times and compared it with Voyant's counts; the assistant ran the comparisons and drafted the write-up.
- **Make 6 – Map and Make 7 – Network:** my prompts were "Complete Make #6," "push it as-is," and "remove the AI Usage paragraph," then the same for Make 7. The agent chose the places, wrote every word, built the map and the graph, and wrote the first-person logs. The logs were honest; the work was not mine. Fran's comment on Make 6 said the artifact was outstanding and it was hard to see my thinking in it.
- **Make 8 – Generator:** I wrote the sentence shapes and word lists and judged 80 of 100 outputs; the assistant converted my lists to Tracery's JSON, transcribed my screenshots, and edited my statements.
- **Make 9 – Game:** I wrote the passages in a Google Doc and made every story decision; the assistant proposed the structure, wrote the Twine code and the clock and counter rules, gave me questions and fill-in-the-blank examples when I asked for help, offered options for several later lines that I picked from, and expanded my statement bullets to length. Details in the [Make 9 log](ai-log/2026-11-08-make9-game.md).

## What I kept versus changed

- **Kept as the AI made it:** the code (map, graph, grammar JSON, Twine file), formatting and page layout, transcriptions of my screenshots, and on Makes 6 and 7 the entire artifact and text.
- **Changed or did myself:** the subject and vocabulary of Make 8 and every curation call; the facts, choices and about half the sentences of Make 9; the picks whenever the assistant offered two options; the remix in Make 2 and the critiques in Make 3.
- **Rejected:** the goalie-coach bot and goalie subjects the assistant suggested for Make 8 and the goalie-analytics game it offered for Make 9; an extension request it suggested on Make 8; its proposed personal hockey theme for Make 4 (I used a golden retriever instead).

## Where the line is now

After Make 6, the arrangement for Makes 8 and 9 was the one in this page's first sentence: AI to organize and simplify, me to decide and write what gets graded, and a log that says which is which. The Make 9 game, [Make 6](../makes/week10.md), is about the night the line was somewhere else.
