# AI Use Log – Make 8 (What Is This Tom Foolery? A Hockey Broadcast Call Generator)

**Date:** November 1, 2026

**Tool Used:** Perplexity Computer (Claude) as a tutor and formatter, in one evening session. The generator itself runs on Tracery (Kate Compton), which is not an AI tool: it is a grammar-based randomizer that fills blanks in sentence shapes from word lists. Google Docs for the plain-text lists and the line-by-line notes. No image, text or music generation by an AI model anywhere in this make.

**Task / Prompt:** This morning my professor's feedback on Make 6 said the AI log made it hard to see my own thinking, because the agent had done the research, picked the places, written the text and built the map. So for Make 8 the arrangement was the other way round. I pasted nine screenshots of the Canvas brief and asked Perplexity to walk me through it and help me choose an idea. It recommended Track A (a Tracery text generator) because the brief is built around generating volume and curating, and I chose the subject, hockey broadcast calls. I then wrote five sentence shapes and ten word lists in plain words in a Google Doc, asked it to convert them into Tracery's JSON format (I kept breaking the syntax in the editor), pasted that into tracery.io/editor and generated 100 outputs in ten batches of ten. I read and annotated 80 of them line by line in the doc. Later I asked it to type out my ten batch screenshots as text, to turn my criteria paragraph into yes/no questions, and to clean up the grammar and order of my process documentation and artist statement. I also asked it, more than once, to do the curation and the writing for me.

**What the AI Suggested:** Reading the brief, it recommended Track A over the chatbot track I had been leaning toward, and it suggested three subjects (post-game quotes, scouting reports, goalie excuses); I went with my own, broadcast calls. It explained what was wrong each time the sample grammar broke (a slot name that was not a list name, a stray string, two closing braces) and warned me that the Tracery tutorial link in the brief now redirects to a spam site. It converted my plain-text lists into JSON, removing spaces from slot names, adding a period to each shape, and setting the faceoff line to repeat the same object (I had not decided match or no-match, and it told me how to switch it). After I generated, it transcribed the 100 lines from my screenshots and counted: 91 unique, 5 repeats, 10 outputs where a team plays itself, and the spread of shapes. When I judged whole batches as keep or reject, it pointed out that my own criteria contradicted the batch verdicts and that the unit had to be the sentence. It rephrased my criteria paragraph into the three questions on the page (Q1, Q2, Q3), using my examples. It ranked my ten keeps by the strength of my own notes ("Perfect," "Best one yet," "Perfection!") and asked me to confirm, and it chose which of my negative notes to show as the duds (all 80 notes are on the page). It pointed out that at #43 and #44 I had praised two own-net lines, the mistake I caught at #65, and suggested saying so in the statement rather than quietly fixing it. It edited my written reflection and my chat comments into the process documentation and artist statement, laid out this page, drafted this log, and made the commit. It declined to do the curation or write the statements, and said why.

**What I Accepted, Changed, or Rejected:** The subject, the five shapes, every word in every list, letting the same team list fill both team slots, generating 100 and reading 80, the three questions, every keep and every dud and the note on each are mine. I accepted Track A, the JSON conversion, the match setting on the faceoff line, the transcription and counts, the question form of my criteria, the ranking of my keeps by my own notes, and the edits to my two statements, which I read before they went live. The sentence about the randomizer never getting tired and the curator getting tired is the one observation in the statement that came from the assistant; I agreed with it and kept it. I did not take the suggestion to ask for an extension, since practice was cancelled and there was time to finish, and I did not take the three subjects it offered.

**Why:** The brief says the point of this make is curation: infinite generation requires selection, and selection is a creative act. Tracery made that literal. The program does not know what it is saying, so every judgment about what was realistic, what was "basically suicide," and what only a goalie would notice had to be mine. Using AI to format JSON, type out screenshots and fix my grammar kept my hands on the parts that are the grade, and this log is meant to make that line easy to see.
