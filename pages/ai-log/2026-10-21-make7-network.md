# AI Use Log – Make 7 (Who Feeds the Model)

**Date:** October 21, 2026

**Tool Used:** Perplexity Computer (Claude) as the assistant that researched, built, tested and drafted the make. Inside it: web search and page reading for the research; a headless Chromium browser (Playwright, not an AI tool) to test the graph at laptop and phone sizes, check keyboard navigation and take the screenshots; Python to assemble the data file and compute degree and betweenness centrality. No image generation. The graph itself runs on D3.js, which is not an AI tool.

**Task / Prompt:** Complete Make 7 (Option B, the AI supply-chain network) and the accessibility workshop, following the Canvas brief. I pasted five screenshots of the assignment page, asked first for a plan and a step-by-step teaching guide, and then said "actually do this for me." Before that I had asked a related question, "How does AI reshape global geographies of power, labor, and environment?", and the assistant's answer drew on my Make 6 map, so the two tasks were connected from the start.

**What the AI Suggested:** The assistant recommended Option B over the other three because Make 6 had already produced 35 sourced places and the money lines on that map could become edges, and it recommended building the graph in D3 on my own site rather than in Kumu so that node size and layout could carry the argument and so the page would be accessible without a third-party embed. It re-used about half of Make 6's sources for the mining, data-centre and labour edges and ran roughly 30 new searches for the middle of the chain (ASML, TSMC, SK hynix, Nvidia's buyers, the Microsoft–OpenAI, Amazon–Anthropic, Google–Anthropic, Meta–Scale AI and Nvidia–OpenAI stakes, Glencore's KCC, Huayou Cobalt, SQM, China Northern Rare Earth, TSMC's Phoenix fab, Sama, Teleperformance, Genpact, Scale). It kept the Make 6 rule that every edge needs a source naming both ends, and it was the assistant that noticed the consequence: the lithium and rare-earth chains become disconnected islands and the cobalt chain touches the main graph through one edge, Huayou to Microsoft, which it marked "contested" because Microsoft disputes the Amnesty finding. It computed the centrality numbers in Python (Nvidia highest degree and betweenness, OpenAI highest in-degree with zero out-degree, Huayou's betweenness above Google's) and those numbers became the spine of the analysis. It designed the two layouts (chain and hub) as two drawings of one dataset, the five edge types with distinct dash patterns, the keyboard and screen-reader support, the text list of connections, dark mode and the sideways-scrolling phone layout, and it measured the colour-contrast ratios. It drafted the network analysis, the artist statement, the process notes, the accessibility statement and this log. During testing it found and fixed three layout problems: a force-directed layout that tangled labels (replaced with fixed columns ordered to reduce crossings), long straight edges that passed through unrelated nodes (replaced with curves), and labels cut off at the left edge.

**What I Accepted, Changed, or Rejected:** I asked for the whole make to be done this way and reviewed the plan before approving it. I kept Option B, the self-built graph, the chain view as the default, the contested label on the Huayou–Microsoft edge and the islands rather than invented links to connect them. Every connection on the graph links to its source, so my checking was spot-checking: opening nodes and following links. The accessibility statement lists my GitHub profile as the contact rather than an email address; the assistant chose that to avoid publishing my email, and I can swap it for an address later.

**Why:** This make is about who is visible in a network and how the drawing itself reveals or hides power, so the honest version has to show whose decisions the drawing reflects. The research, the code, the metrics and the first draft of every text came from the assistant. The standard I held it to was the same as in Make 6: every line traceable to a named source, every gap stated rather than smoothed over, and the industry's own picture (the hub view) drawn as fairly as the counter-picture, so a reader can see that the difference between them is a decision and not a fact.
