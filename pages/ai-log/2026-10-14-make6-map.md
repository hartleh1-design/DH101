# AI Use Log – Make 6 (The Cloud Has a Body)

**Date:** October 14, 2026

**Tool Used:** Perplexity Computer (Claude) as the assistant that researched, built, tested and drafted the make. Inside it: web search and page reading for the research; a headless Chromium browser (Playwright, not an AI tool) to test the map at laptop and phone sizes and take the screenshots. No image generation. The map itself runs on Leaflet, MapLibre GL and OpenFreeMap tiles, none of which are AI tools.

**Task / Prompt:** Build a digital map of AI's infrastructures with two or three layers, 15 or more locations, research documentation, an artist statement and a sources list, following the Make 6 brief. My prompt was the brief itself (four screenshots of the Canvas page) and the instruction "Complete Make #6," which the assistant read alongside my earlier makes and AI logs so the page would match how I have documented the others.

**What the AI Suggested:** The assistant proposed building the map by hand with Leaflet on my GitHub Pages site instead of in Google My Maps, so the symbols, sizes and views could carry the argument. It chose three of the four layers from the brief (mines, data centres, labelling and moderation labour) and kept the headquarters as a small reference layer. It set a rule for every pin, one number, one person, one source, and then ran about 90 searches and read roughly 70 reports and articles (USGS, Ireland's CSO, Virginia's JLARC, Global Witness, Amnesty, Epoch AI, AP, Reuters, TIME, Rest of World, the Bureau of Investigative Journalism, Mongabay, Context, BBC, DW, OPB and others) to fill 35 pins in 22 countries, checking each coordinate against Wikipedia. It suggested the title, the two switchable views ("how the industry maps AI" against the counter-map), the dashed money lines, and the rule that a line only appears where a source names one of the five companies, plus a note on why most mines have no line. It flagged that the mining sources are about phones and cars rather than AI and labelled those pins "hardware link" instead of hiding the gap. It drafted the "What is missing" list, the research documentation, the artist statement, the sources list and the first version of this log. When the first basemap provider (CARTO) started returning "API key required" tiles mid-build, it switched to OpenFreeMap and added a fallback for browsers without WebGL.

**What I Accepted, Changed, or Rejected:** I asked for the whole make to be done this way and reviewed the page and the map before approving the push to GitHub. I kept the self-built map, the three layers, the counter-map as the default view and the honesty labels on the mining pins. I kept Anthropic and its Indiana data centre on the map because the assistant runs on Claude, so the tool that made the page is one of the things the page is about. Everything on a pin links to its source, so my checking was spot-checking: opening pins and following links.

**Why:** This make is about data visualization as argument, so the honest version has to show whose decisions the argument reflects. Most of the research and all of the code came from the assistant; the standard I held it to was that every number on the map had to be traceable to a named source and every gap had to be stated rather than smoothed over. Where the connection to AI is interpretive (the mines), the map says so instead of pretending the sources say more than they do.
